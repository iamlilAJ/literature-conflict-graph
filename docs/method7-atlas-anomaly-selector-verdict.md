# Method 7 — Atlas as anomaly-selector — verdict

**Date:** 2026-05-31
**Method 7 of:** the /loop "test different methods" plan. Tests the standing memo [[atlas-bottleneck-align-tested]]'s claim that Atlas's value is anomaly-selection.
**One-line answer:** **No measurable benefit.** Joint anomalies (Atlas-selected, 484-candidate set) and aigraph-native non-joint anomalies produce essentially identical hypothesis distributions when run through the same Workflow C (forward-frame + reflect). All 5 Likert axes Δ ≤ 0.15. Joint-vs-native is a tie. **Atlas's only confirmed value as a system input is as the evaluator from Method 3, not as an anomaly source.**

## Experiment

- Workflow controlled: both populations generated by Workflow C (forward + reflect), production winner.
- Population J — **joint anomalies, Atlas-selected**: the 16 anomalies sampled stratified across bottleneck dimensions in Method 2. Took Arm P outputs (`hP` from `m2_out.jsonl`) — same anomalies, Atlas context *stripped* from the prompt to isolate the source-vs-context confound. After Method 2 errors: n=10.
- Population N — **aigraph-native non-joint anomalies**: the 16 anomalies from `genexp16.json` (sampled from val1-primary, no Atlas overlap). Took Workflow C outputs (`hC` from `genexp16_out.jsonl`). After errors: n=16, with 12 of those clearing the Likert judge.
- Same Likert judge as Method 3 (`run_method3_judge.py` unchanged) — 5 axes, no new code.
- 27 judge inputs, 22 ok, 5 errors. Kimi-K2.6, 4 workers, ~6 min wall.

## Results (Likert 1–5, n=10 joint + 12 native)

| criterion | joint | native | Δ J−N |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| atlas_overlap | 3.30 | 3.25 | +0.05 |
| forward_looking | 4.60 | 4.50 | +0.10 |
| named_mechanism | 4.90 | 5.00 | −0.10 |
| single_variable_test | 5.00 | 5.00 | 0.00 |
| specific_scope | 4.90 | 4.75 | +0.15 |

`atlas_overlap` distribution:
- joint: `{4: 3, 3: 7}` — 30% at "directly addresses known bottleneck"
- native: `{4: 3, 3: 9}` — 25% at "directly addresses known bottleneck"

3 hits at overlap=4 in each population. Atlas-selected anomalies do not produce more bottleneck-anchored hyps.

## Reading

1. **Both populations saturate near the Likert ceiling** (named_mechanism 4.9-5.0, single_variable_test 5.0). The reflect step in Workflow C is the dominant lever — it polishes hypotheses to high specificity regardless of where the anomaly came from. Method 3's *non-reflected* forward population scored named_mechanism 4.67 by contrast; the reflect adds ~0.3 across the board.

2. **`atlas_overlap` similar across populations** is the cleanest evidence against the selector hypothesis. If Atlas-selected anomalies were inherently more "real-bottleneck-shaped", their hyps should score higher overlap. They don't (3.30 vs 3.25).

3. **The `pair_why` for overlap=4 cases is symmetric.** Joint examples praise the hyp as "directly maps bottleneck [N]'s observation", "directly targets the sparse large outlier problem", "aligns with bottleneck [0]". Native examples praise "Both center on accuracy degradation", "Both center on how discretization step size", "directly instantiates bottleneck [1]'s open problem". No qualitative difference.

## Implication for the /loop

After 4 iterations, the answer to "how does Atlas data give best effect for our system" is **narrow but real**:

| Atlas use | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Atlas as prompt context | 2 | **Hurts** (P beats A 7:4 pair) |
| Atlas as anomaly source / selector | 7 | **No effect** (Δ ≤ 0.15 all axes) |
| **Atlas as novelty/anchoring evaluator** | **3** | **Works** (+0.25 overlap discriminates populations) |

The clean answer: **wire `atlas_overlap` into delivery as a per-hypothesis runtime score**, surface it to end-users alongside the existing rendered hyp. This needs no thaw, costs ~1 Kimi call per delivered hyp, and gives end-users a real-valued specificity-and-anchoring signal we don't have today. Tactical next step is Method 9 — engineering, not a new test method.

The other potentially useful Atlas use we haven't tested: Atlas conflict-graph edges as a hypothesis `graph_bridge` validator. The 484-candidate joint anomaly set is one slice of the Atlas conflict graph; the full 9.6M-edge graph could potentially score whether a generated `graph_bridge` matches an existing complementary relation. That's Method 10, deferred.

## Honest caveats

1. **n=10 vs n=12 is small.** A 50/50 version (~30 min Kimi at 4 workers) would tighten CIs.
2. **The native population came from val1-primary which has no post-2024 papers.** Their topic distribution may differ systematically from the joint population (which is drawn from the 540p corpus).
3. **Population J anomalies were Atlas-selected but their prompts had Atlas context stripped** — to isolate source from context. If the "joint anomaly" framing in `central_question` itself is doing useful work (as some prompt prefix), Method 7 strips that and may understate Atlas's value.
4. **Workflow C's reflect step ceilings everything.** Method 7 might detect different distributions on a *non-reflected* baseline; we already have Method 3 evidence forward-only-no-reflect didn't ceiling.
